# TMUA in six weeks

> **Verify the dates yourself before anything else.** Registration and test
> dates are set by the exam board each cycle and this file is not the source
> of truth. In recent cycles registration has closed in **late September**
> with the test in **mid-October** — which means the deadline is the nearest
> thing on this page. Check the official TMUA page and your school's exams
> officer this week. A perfect six weeks of prep is worth nothing if the
> registration lapses.

Also confirm on each university's own course page that TMUA is actually
required for the courses being applied to. Requirements shift year to year.

---

## The shape of it

Two papers, 75 minutes each, 20 multiple-choice questions each, no
calculator.

- **Paper 1 — Applications of Mathematical Knowledge.** Familiar AS-level
  pure content, but the questions are angled so that the obvious method is
  too slow. The skill is *choosing* a method.
- **Paper 2 — Mathematical Reasoning.** Logic and proof. Negation,
  contrapositive, converse, necessary vs sufficient, counterexamples,
  contradiction. Most people are weaker here and most people under-prepare
  for it, which makes it the better place to spend marginal hours.

Roughly 3 minutes 45 seconds per question. That number should feel
uncomfortable. It's meant to.

---

## Week 0 — this week (3 days)

1. **Register.** Before anything else.
2. **Sit a full past paper cold.** Both papers, timed, no notes, no
   calculator. It will feel bad. That's the point — this is the baseline,
   not a performance.
3. **Mark it and log every error** in `log/errors.md`. Categorise each one:
   *knowledge gap*, *method choice*, *arithmetic slip*, *misread*, *ran out
   of time*. The distribution tells you what the next five weeks are for.

Do not skip step 2 to "revise first". You cannot plan without the baseline.

## Weeks 1–2 — Paper 1 content

Target the gaps the diagnostic found, in this rough order of TMUA weight:

- Algebra: quadratics, discriminants, inequalities, surds, indices
- Functions: domain/range, composition, inverses, transformations
- Graphs: sketching, asymptotes, intersections, symmetry
- Sequences and series: arithmetic, geometric, sigma notation, binomial
- Logs and exponentials: laws, solving, change of base
- Trigonometry: identities, equations, exact values, radians
- Coordinate geometry: lines, circles, tangents
- Differentiation and integration: stationary points, areas, basic rules

**Daily rhythm (60–90 min):**
- 10 min — recall drill (`drill/`), yesterday's weak topics
- 40 min — 8–10 timed questions on one topic
- 15 min — review, log errors, ask the tutor about *method*, not answers
- Optional 15 min — code an exploration (see below)

## Weeks 3–4 — Paper 2 reasoning

This is where the marks are hiding. Work through `plan/paper2-reasoning.md`.

Same daily rhythm, but the review step changes: for every wrong answer,
name the *type* of logical error, not just the correction. Errors repeat by
type, and naming them is how you stop repeating them.

## Week 5 — full timed papers

Two full papers a week under real conditions. Same time of day as the real
test if you can. Mark, log, and spend the following session only on the
errors — no new content.

## Week 6 — taper

No new material. Re-read the error log end to end. Redo the ten problems
you got wrong most stubbornly. Short sessions. Sleep.

---

## The one rule that makes this work

**Attempt first, alone, on paper, timed. Bring Claude in afterwards.**

Claude is for reviewing method and closing gaps, not for getting through
problems. The `CLAUDE.md` in this folder enforces that — it will refuse to
hand over answers — but the discipline has to come from you.
